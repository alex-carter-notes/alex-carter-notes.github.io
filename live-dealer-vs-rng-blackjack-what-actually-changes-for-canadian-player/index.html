The rules on the felt look similar, but the experience, the pace and sometimes the numbers behind the game are not. This guide explains the differences in plain language so you can choose the format that fits your budget and temperament.

How the cards are dealt

An RNG table uses software to simulate a shuffled shoe. The result of every hand is generated electronically, and independent testing labs audit the generator on regulated sites. A live dealer table uses real decks or a real shoe, shuffled by a real dealer or an automatic shuffler, and streams the game from a studio.

Neither format is "due" to give you a win after a losing streak. Cards have no memory in either case. What differs is how often the shoe is reshuffled and how many decks are in it, and both of those are worth checking in the rules panel.

Pace and how many hands you play

This is the biggest practical difference. An RNG table can deal a hand in seconds, so you can easily play several hundred hands in an hour. A live table waits for every player to act, so the average falls to somewhere near 60 hands per hour or fewer when the table is busy.

More hands per hour means a larger expected loss per hour at the same stake, because the house edge applies to every hand. If your goal is to make a small budget last, the slower live format usually stretches it further. If you want to practise strategy, the faster RNG version gives you more repetitions.

Rules and payouts to compare

The usual rules still decide the edge more than the format:

  • Blackjack payout. 3 to 2 is the standard to look for. 6 to 5 is noticeably worse.
  • Dealer on soft 17. Standing is better for you than hitting.
  • Number of decks. Fewer decks help slightly.
  • Doubling and splitting. Doubling after a split and re-splitting aces are both player-friendly.

Live tables sometimes offer special rules, such as seat limits or fixed side bets, so read each table individually rather than assuming it matches the RNG version of the game.

Limits and bankroll

Minimum bets are often lower at RNG tables, which makes them easier on small budgets, while live tables tend to start higher and have a wider range of limits. Pick the table where the minimum bet is a small fraction of your session budget; a common guide is to hold 40 to 50 betting units.

Side bets and bonuses

Both formats may carry side bets such as perfect pairs or 21+3. These pay well when they hit but carry a much higher house edge than the main game, so they are best treated as entertainment, not strategy. Which one to choose

Choose RNG if you want fast practice, low minimum bets and no waiting. Choose live if you prefer a slower pace, a social feel and the sense of a real table. Either way, set a deposit limit and a session reminder before you start, and stop when you reach them.
